## Picking the right track

[Commercial disputes need a forum that matches the stakes, the counterparty, and the commercial outcome the client actually wants. Choosing the wrong track early can cost time, money, and leverage.]

### Litigation in the Ghanaian courts

[The High Court of Ghana has broad jurisdiction over civil and commercial matters, with appeals to the Court of Appeal and then the Supreme Court. It is open, public, and for many disputes — especially where urgent injunctive relief or third-party discovery is needed — it is the only viable route.]

**Best for:** [matters where you need interim relief, state enforcement of orders, or the counterparty is judgment-proof without court process.]
**Watch for:** [case backlog, public nature of proceedings, and longer timelines for complex matters.]

### Domestic arbitration under Act 798

[The Arbitration Act, 2010 (Act 798) provides a modern arbitration framework, and the Ghana Alternative Dispute Resolution Centre (GADRC) and other institutions administer domestic arbitrations with published fee scales.]

**Best for:** [contractual disputes where confidentiality matters, the parties are sophisticated, and you want a single final process without the possibility of a second appeal on the facts.]
**Watch for:** [enforcement of the award is through the courts anyway, so ensure the counterparty has attachable assets.]

### Mediation — early and often

[Mediation is a facilitated negotiation. It is not a decision-making process, so it only produces an outcome if both sides agree. But it is by far the fastest and cheapest path for matters where there is still room to negotiate.]

**Best for:** [parties with an ongoing commercial relationship who want to resolve the issue privately, or where the legal merits are relatively evenly balanced.]
**Watch for:** [don't mediate too late — the best time to mediate is often before proceedings are issued, when positions are not yet hardened.]

### A practical recommendation

[We usually recommend a staged approach: an early case assessment followed by an offer of mediation or pre-action settlement discussions; formal proceedings or arbitration if those fail; and continued willingness to settle up to and including the day of the hearing.
